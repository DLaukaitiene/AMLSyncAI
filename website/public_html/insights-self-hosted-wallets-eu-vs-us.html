Self-hosted wallets: the US steps back, the EU tightens

9 October 2026 · Deimena Laukaitienė, Founder

On 5 October 2026 the US Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) withdrew two crypto proposals. One would have required ID checks and record-keeping for unhosted wallet transfers over $3,000. The other targeted crypto mixers.

The EU has gone the other way

  • Since December 2024, under the EU Transfer of Funds Regulation, a CASP sending more than €1,000 to a self-hosted wallet must assess whether its client owns or controls that wallet.
  • From 10 July 2027, the AMLR also bans anonymous crypto accounts, including those using anonymity-enhancing coins.

What this means in practice

Lithuanian CASPs and EMIs with US partners will see the same flows treated differently on each side of the Atlantic. A partner's lighter requirements do not change yours: your own files still have to meet the EU standard.

Useful questions to ask now:

  • How do you verify self-hosted wallet ownership today – and is the method documented and applied consistently?
  • Can you evidence the check for every transfer above the threshold?
  • Are you ready to identify and exit anonymous or anonymity-enhancing exposures before July 2027?
