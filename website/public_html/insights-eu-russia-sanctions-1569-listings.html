EU adds 1,569 names to Russia sanctions list: what it means for screening teams

26 October 2026 · Deimena Laukaitienė, Founder

On 12 October 2026 the EU added 1,569 names to its Russia sanctions list – the largest set of listings it has ever agreed in one go. They are 826 companies from Russia's military-industrial sector and 743 of their CEOs.

Why this matters for screening teams

For screening teams at Lithuanian banks, electronic money institutions (EMIs) and crypto-asset service providers (CASPs), a list update of this size means a wave of new potential matches – both in real-time payment screening and in re-screening of the existing customer base.

  • Most alerts will be false positives – common names, transliteration variants, similar company names.
  • Each one still needs a documented decision. Supervisors expect to see why an alert was discounted, not just that it was closed.
  • Ownership and control count too. Companies owned or controlled by a listed person are covered by the restrictions even if they do not appear on the list themselves – so a clean list check is not the end of the analysis.

Practical steps after a large list update

  1. Confirm your screening provider has loaded the new listings and re-run screening across the full customer and counterparty base.
  2. Prioritise alerts by exposure – active customers, open payments and high-risk corridors first.
  3. Apply a consistent, written approach to ownership-and-control checks for corporate customers.
  4. Track backlog and ageing daily so true matches are not stuck behind hundreds of false positives.
