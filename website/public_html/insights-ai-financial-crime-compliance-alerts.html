AI in financial crime compliance: why people still handle most alert work

20 October 2026 · Deimena Laukaitienė, Founder

A new FinCrime Frontier Report from SymphonyAI and AML Intelligence (16 October 2026) looks at how far firms have actually got with AI in financial crime compliance. The answer: further in ambition than in practice.

The numbers

  • 62% of firms say AI and automation are their top investment priority.
  • 82% are still at or below the pilot stage.
  • 76% review alerts manually or with only partial automation.
  • In 71% of firms, 5% or fewer of investigated alerts end in a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR).

Most alert work leads nowhere – but it still has to be done properly

If 95% or more of investigated alerts do not lead to a report, the bulk of a monitoring team's week goes on alerts that are ultimately closed. Every one of them still has to be reviewed and documented to a standard a supervisor or auditor will accept.

For banks, EMIs and CASPs, that means people will be handling most alert work for a while yet. AI will reduce the load over time, but most firms are not there today – and backlogs do not wait for technology roadmaps.

Keeping queues under control in the meantime

  • Measure alert volumes, ageing and closure rates weekly.
  • Tune rules and thresholds where false-positive rates are clearly out of line.
  • Plan extra review capacity for peaks instead of letting backlogs build.
