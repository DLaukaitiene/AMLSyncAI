AMLR Article 75: AMLA and EDPB guidelines on information-sharing partnerships

4 November 2026 · Deimena Laukaitienė, Founder

The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA) and the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) are drafting joint guidelines on information-sharing partnerships under Article 75 of the AMLR. A public consultation is planned for the first half of 2027.

What Article 75 allows

From 10 July 2027, obliged entities such as banks, electronic money institutions (EMIs) and crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) can share customer and transaction information with each other within a partnership – but only where it is strictly necessary for customer due diligence (CDD) and suspicious transaction reporting.

The conditions before joining

  1. Notify your supervisor before taking part in a partnership.
  2. Carry out a data protection impact assessment (DPIA).
  3. Write an information-sharing policy setting out what is shared, why and how.
  4. Record every exchange of information.
  5. No client decision can rest on shared data alone – firms still need their own assessment.

Why it matters for Lithuanian firms

For Lithuanian payment institutions, EMIs and CASPs dealing with scam and authorised push payment fraud, partnerships could help spot mule accounts and fraud patterns that no single firm can see on its own.

The flip side is preparation: governance, data protection documentation and record-keeping need to be in place before any data is shared. Firms considering joining should start mapping their CDD and reporting processes against Article 75 now, rather than waiting for the final guidelines.
