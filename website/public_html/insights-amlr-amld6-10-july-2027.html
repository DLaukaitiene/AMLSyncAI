AMLR & AMLD6: what changes on 10 July 2027

30 October 2026 · Deimena Laukaitienė, Founder

On 10 July 2027, anti-money laundering rules across the European Union change fundamentally. Most of the detailed obligations that today sit in each country's national AML law are replaced by a single, directly applicable regulation. On the same day, Member States must have transposed the new AML directive into national law.

For compliance teams at payment and e-money institutions, crypto-asset service providers, banks, brokers and gaming operators, the practical question is simple: is your team ready, or still working under the old framework?

The two instruments

  1. AMLR – Regulation (EU) 2024/1624. The EU "single rulebook" for obliged entities. As a regulation, it applies directly – no national transposition, and far less room for country-by-country differences in customer due diligence (CDD), beneficial ownership or reporting.
  2. AMLD6 – Directive (EU) 2024/1640. Rules for the national side of the system: supervisors, Financial Intelligence Units (FIUs) and central registers.

Key AMLR changes for obliged entities

  1. €10,000 EU-wide cash payment limit for traders of goods and services. Member States can keep or set a lower limit.
  2. Beneficial ownership at 25% or more. One harmonised threshold for identifying beneficial owners through ownership.
  3. Fixed CDD update cycles: customer information must be refreshed at least every year for high-risk customers and at least every five years for all others.
  4. No anonymous accounts – the ban explicitly covers anonymous crypto-asset accounts.
  5. Enhanced measures for very high-net-worth clients holding €50 million or more in assets.
  6. Wider scope: crypto-asset service providers, crowdfunding service providers and traders in luxury goods are covered, and professional football clubs and agents join from 2029.
  7. Board-level accountability: a member of the management body must be responsible for AML compliance.

Key AMLD6 changes at national level

  1. Beneficial ownership registers open to anyone with a legitimate interest, with stronger powers for register authorities to verify the data they hold.
  2. FIUs can suspend transactions and accounts when they suspect money laundering or terrorist financing.
  3. National bank account registers interconnected through a single EU access point by 2029.

What this means for your compliance team

Most firms will not need a new AML programme – but almost every firm will need to update one. In practice, that means:

  • Gap analysis of current policies and procedures against the AMLR, article by article.
  • CDD remediation: checking that existing customer files meet the new refresh cycles, beneficial ownership rules and enhanced due diligence triggers.
  • Updated risk assessments and governance documents reflecting management-body accountability.
  • Training so analysts apply the new rules consistently from day one.

The work tends to land on teams that are already busy with day-to-day onboarding, monitoring and reviews. Planning capacity early – rather than in spring 2027 – is the difference between an orderly transition and a backlog.
